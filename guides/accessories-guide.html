ACCESSORIES · BUYING GUIDE

Choose accessories you'll actually use

A good accessory should solve a real problem: make work more comfortable, improve what you can see or hear, or make everyday tasks easier.

1. Monitors: start with your desk and workload

Screen size and resolution affect how much you can see. A 24-inch Full HD monitor is a common basic option; 27-inch 1440p is a popular balance for sharper text and workspace. Higher resolution needs more graphics performance in games.

  • Office and study: Prioritise readable text, adjustable height, suitable brightness and eye-level positioning.
  • Gaming: Check refresh rate, response-time behaviour, adaptive sync and reviews for motion clarity.
  • Creative work: Check colour coverage, accuracy, uniformity and calibration options.

Also check the monitor's inputs, stand adjustment and whether your laptop or GPU supports the desired resolution and refresh rate over that connection.

2. Keyboards: comfort comes first

Try to understand the layout, key spacing, switch feel and noise before buying. Mechanical keyboards are not automatically more comfortable than membrane keyboards. Consider whether you need a number pad, function row, compact layout, wireless connection or multiple-device switching.

3. Mice: shape matters more than marketing

A mouse should fit your hand and grip style. Check its shape, weight, side buttons, sensor performance and scroll wheel. For gaming, compare reliable sensor tests and click latency; for office use, comfort and scroll behaviour may matter more.

4. Headphones: decide where you'll use them

  • Calls and classes: Prioritise microphone clarity, comfort and reliable connectivity.
  • Travel: Active noise cancellation can help with steady background noise, but fit and battery life still matter.
  • Gaming: Comfort, positional audio, microphone quality and connection stability are useful checks.
  • Music: Listen if possible. Sound preference is personal, and specification sheets do not tell the whole story.

5. Wired or wireless?

Wired accessories avoid charging and can offer consistent connections. Wireless accessories reduce cable clutter and are convenient, but check battery life, charging method, latency and whether they can be used while charging.

6. Check compatibility and warranty

Confirm the connection type your device supports: USB-A, USB-C, Bluetooth, HDMI, DisplayPort or a 3.5 mm audio jack. For wireless devices, check supported Bluetooth features and operating-system compatibility. Review the warranty, service process and seller return policy.

Simple rule: Spend more where comfort or daily use genuinely improves. A reliable mid-range accessory is often a better choice than an expensive model with features you will never use.
