How to choose a laptop

The right laptop depends on what you do with it. Learn which specifications matter, which ones are easy to misunderstand and what to check before paying.

Start here: Decide your main use and budget before comparing model numbers. A laptop for coding and college has different priorities from one for gaming, video editing or travel.

1. Choose for your actual workload

  • College and everyday use: Prioritise a good screen, comfortable keyboard, reliable battery and at least 16 GB RAM if your budget allows.
  • Programming: Look for a capable modern processor, 16 GB RAM or more, a comfortable keyboard and upgradeable storage where possible. Heavy Android development, virtual machines and compiling benefit from more memory.
  • Gaming and 3D work: A dedicated GPU matters. Check the exact GPU model and its laptop power limit, not just the name printed on the box.
  • Video editing: Balance CPU, GPU, RAM, screen quality and storage. Your software and footage resolution affect what you need.

2. Understand the processor

The CPU handles general computing tasks. Compare the exact processor model and generation rather than relying only on labels such as i5, i7, Ryzen 5 or Ryzen 7. Different generations and power limits can perform very differently.

For a fair comparison, look for independent reviews that test the exact laptop. Cooling and power settings influence sustained performance, especially during long workloads.

3. RAM: how much is enough?

RAM is the short-term workspace used by open applications. Too little can make multitasking and demanding software feel slow.

  • 8 GB: Basic use only; increasingly limiting for a new laptop.
  • 16 GB: A sensible starting point for general use, college and many development tasks.
  • 32 GB or more: Useful for virtual machines, large projects, heavier editing and demanding multitasking.

Check whether RAM is soldered, replaceable or partly upgradeable. Also check the maximum supported capacity before buying.

4. Storage: prefer an SSD

An SSD makes booting and opening applications much faster than an old mechanical hard drive. For most buyers, 512 GB is a more comfortable starting point than 256 GB, especially if games, development tools or media files are involved. Check whether the laptop has an extra M.2 slot or whether the existing drive can be replaced.

5. Graphics: integrated or dedicated?

Integrated graphics are built into the processor and are usually suitable for everyday work, streaming and light creative tasks. A dedicated GPU is useful for modern games, GPU-accelerated editing, 3D applications and some compute workloads.

For gaming laptops, compare real game benchmarks and GPU wattage. Two laptops with the same GPU name can deliver different performance because of cooling and power limits.

6. Do not ignore the display

Check resolution, brightness, colour coverage, refresh rate and panel type. A high refresh rate is useful for smoother motion, but it does not automatically mean the screen has accurate colours or good brightness. If you work outdoors, brightness matters. If you edit photos or video, colour coverage and accuracy matter more.

7. Battery, weight and ports

Look for independent battery tests rather than trusting only the manufacturer's maximum claim. Check the actual port selection: USB-A, USB-C, HDMI, audio and charging support. USB-C ports can have different capabilities, so confirm whether a port supports display output, high-speed data or USB Power Delivery.

8. Check these before buying in India

  • Confirm the exact model number and configuration; similar names can hide different screens, RAM or GPUs.
  • Read reviews of the exact model, especially for temperatures, fan noise, battery life and build quality.
  • Check the warranty terms, service availability and return/replacement policy of the seller.
  • Compare the final price across trusted retailers, including bank offers and exchange conditions.
  • Do not pay extra for a specification you will not use.

Simple rule: Buy for your heaviest regular workload, but do not overspend for a rare task. A balanced laptop is usually a better purchase than one impressive specification surrounded by compromises.
